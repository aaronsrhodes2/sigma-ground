# Holodeck fidelity: real masses and makeup

Generated by `stage/gmod/holodeck/fidelity.py` from `data/fidelity_research.json` (the sourced reference table), `data/fidelity_specs.json` (which real family each model is) and the engine's own geometry (`data/measured.json`); do not edit by hand. The numbers are written to `data/fidelity.json` and carried into the catalog as `mass`, `engine_mass`, `mass_source` by `build_catalog.py`. Nothing in the engine was changed: the complexity audit only reports.

## What was done

Half-Life 2 gave every prop a gameplay mass (a locker 2000 kg, a stove 1500 kg, a bathtub 900 kg, a mug 1 to 4 kg). Each of the 127 library models was looked at on its photograph (`data/sheets_final`), assigned the real-world family it is (an oak cask or a steel drum, a two-seat or three-seat sofa, a clawfoot tub or a steel-enamel one), and given the mass of a sourced reference item of that family, scaled to the model's size and held inside the family's sourced range. Where the sources do not describe a model, the engine mass is kept and the model is labelled unaudited; nothing is invented.

- mass = clamp(reference mass x s^k, low, high), with s = (the model's bounding-box volume / the reference's)^(1/3) the linear scale factor and k its exponent: **3** for solids and frame-and-panel objects (mass follows volume) and **2** for thin shells (geometrically similar shells at constant wall thickness scale with surface area). That is mass proportional to V^1 and V^(2/3). low and high are the family's sourced range.
- A family or model marked *fixed* skips the scaling where the bounding box is not a fair measure (an umbrella canopy, a lamp arm, foliage, a high cistern, a flue).
- If the scaled mass is more than 2x outside the sourced range the model is left unaudited and the extrapolation only recorded.
- Weights are net or product weights where the sources give them. Package, shipping and unstated weights are marked as such in each reference (`weight_kind`) and were not preferred over a net figure.
- Confidence: high: evidence 'opened', a good fit, no clamp and the model's size used; medium: one step down; low: two or more steps down. Steps down: evidence 'mixed' (1), 'search-summary' or 'single-source' (2), a partial fit (1), the scaled mass clamped to a bound (1), a fixed scale, which does not use the model's size (1).

## Headline

- 127 models: **121 audited** (mass replaced by a sourced figure), **6 unaudited** (engine mass kept, reasons below).
- Confidence of the 121 audited: 9 high, 45 medium, 67 low; 45 were held at a sourced bound because the model lies outside the sizes the sources describe.
- The engine mass is a median 3.5x the sourced mass, at most 86x.

## Complexity audit (report only)

The engine's collision body (`convexes`, `triangles` from `measured.json`) against the rigid pieces a faithful decomposition of the real object needs, estimated from each family's sourced construction text (a chair has legs, a seat and a back; a bookcase is an open carcass with shelves): the low end is the major rigid bodies any faithful decomposition needs, the high end counts every distinct member (legs, rails, slats, shelves) on its own. A closed object (doors and drawers shut) is fine as one convex hull; an object with an open interior or underside is not, because a single hull makes it a solid block that nothing can stand in or under, and the engine derives its inertia from that hull as if it were solid. Caveats the sources themselves record:

- `sofa_3seat`: Sleepers, sectionals and reclining sofas are heavier and are not covered by this range.
- `chair`: A single chair class; the sourced chairs span bentwood, moulded-shell and steel stack chairs, so the range is wide.
- `stool_wood`: Artek Stool 60 is 3.2 kg per Connox but 6.8 kg per dimensions.com for the same stool: a direct conflict the researcher could not resolve.
- `bench_park_steel`: Every park-bench figure is from a search summary that could not be opened (certificate error, 403, timeouts). The research's 80 kg high leaves out the Global Industrial 200 to 242 lb (91 to 110 kg) listings its own note names, so heavier park benches exist.
- `hospital_bed`: The sourced beds are modern powered beds; the engine model is an older crank-style frame.
- `table_dining`: The solid-wood figures (Woood, vidaXL, Kare) are search summaries; the retailer pages timed out.
- `side_table_light`: Heavier wood or metal side tables are unsourced: this class understates a solid-wood nightstand.
- `meeting_table`: The engine model is 5.83 m long, 1.6 times the longest sourced table; no table that large is sourced.
- `patio_table_alu`: A teak or acacia patio table is heavier; no verified solid-wood patio table figure exists.
- `bookshelf`: Empty-bookcase mass; the books the models show are not included. Solid-wood bookcases are heavier and unsourced.
- `wardrobe`: A solid-wood armoire is not covered (the research says so) and is heavier than particleboard.
- `locker_steel`: A single tier is sourced; the engine models are banks of several lockers and one open two-door locker, so they extrapolate (the one sourced bank of three is about 63 kg, a search summary).
- `crate_wood`: Pallet mass is unsourced and included in the reference weights, so the crates here read slightly heavy.
- `box_cardboard`: Empty-box mass; contents (the files in the file box) are not included.
- `cask_oak`: Empty mass; a full cask is about 227 kg.
- `chest`: No weight for an authentic wooden military footlocker was found; the cedar chest is the nearest sourced case.
- `floor_lamp`: Mass is dominated by the base weight, so it is scaled with the volume, not the pole's surface.
- `desk_lamp`: Low and high are single listings: soft bounds.
- `fridge_cabinet`: A compressor and the foam layer are a large part of the real mass, so the shell-only cross-check is expected to come out well below the sourced mass.
- `sink_steel_kitchen`: The engine's kitchen sink is enamelled, not stainless; an enamelled sink is heavier, so this likely understates it.
- `toilet_bowl_only`: A floor-standing elongated bowl is heavier than a wall-hung one: likely an overestimate.
- `bathtub_cast_iron`: The steel-enamel (about 50 kg) and acrylic (about 35 kg) tubs are other families; the models are clawfoot tubs.
- `radiator_cast_iron`: Two opened listings disagree by 2.4 times, so this is a wide, low-confidence class.
- `stove_cast_iron_small`: The research's typical 170 kg is a mid-size steel-plate stove (Drolet), not a small cast-iron one.
- `display_counter_glass`: Use as a rough target only (the research says so); a refrigerated deli case with a compressor is heavier.
- `phone_corded_modern`: The research's own Model 500 rotary figures (1.8 kg) describe a different object and were not used.
- `potted_plant`: One retailer family, so not two independent sources; the research says so.

## Honest limits

- **Evidence.** `opened` means the reference figure was read on an opened page; `mixed` an opened reference with bounds from search summaries or a researcher's own pick; `search-summary` and `single-source` mean the figures could not be checked on a page or come from one maker. The confidence column follows from this, not from the fit alone.
- **Researcher's picks.** Several 'typical' masses are the researcher's own pick inside the sourced range (each says so in `mass_basis`); they are design inputs, not measurements.
- **Bounding boxes lie for open objects.** Scaling by box volume treats a chair or a lamp as if it filled its box; the clamp to the sourced range and the *fixed* exceptions are what keep it honest, and the held-at-a-bound list above shows where they bit.
- **Thin shells.** Wall thickness is the weak point of nearly every family (the sources rarely publish it); where it is sourced, the cross-check uses it.
- **What the models contain.** Masses are of the empty object: books, files and drink are not included, and a mattress is only included where a family says so.
- **Not covered.** Friction, elasticity, damping, the centre of mass and the inertia tensor are not sourced. The engine derives inertia from its collision hull and the mass, so a hollow object whose hull is a solid block has too small a moment of inertia for its mass (see the complexity audit).
- **Mentat.** Densities come from Mentat (`fidelity_specs.json`, re-checkable with `--verify-densities`). It has none for steel, foam, fabric, ceramic or cardboard; the masses of furniture are not physics Mentat computes, so they rest on the research table, not on Mentat.
