OPERATIC PLAY, SCENE 27: THE CLOCK IS THE EXHIBIT
(sigma-ground, the night of 2026-11-02 into 2026-11-03)

Status: RESOLVED. Related: the session log entry of the same night (live body_position, C29).

## The Problem

Mentat's body_position had two faces. For a date in the past it read JPL's kernel, which is right: a kernel is a measured fact. For a date in the
future it read a table we had sealed on 2026-10-27, thirty years of our own integration stored on the data volume, and interpolated. The table was
honest about what it was, carried a fingerprint of the physics that built it and refused to serve if a byte had changed, but it was still a copy of
an old calculation. The Captain's rule that afternoon ended the second face: when sigma-ground is asked where a body is, the answer is our own
calculation computed then. Stored data is allowed only for what is known fact, the JPL kernels. The sealed table stays as a record to be judged later.

The obstacle was time. A year of the reduced model, eleven bodies and 386 prescribed sources with relativity, zonals, tides and the Moon's figure, cost
fifteen to twenty-five minutes at the fine Forest-Ruth step, and the question "where is Mars now" is already 2.8 years past the edge of DE442's data.
A chat tool that hangs for an hour is not a tool.

## The Investigation

The force routine was the Python it had always been: double loops over the bodies for Newton, the Einstein-Infeld-Hoffmann cross terms, the three
zonals with their reactions and the static tide. Measured under tonight's load (the contest and the lever arms had every core busy), one evaluation
cost 6.5 ms, of which the EIH loops were 4.7 ms and the already vectorised prescribed sources 0.13 ms. Yoshida-6 at 0.1 d needs 28 evaluations a step
(fourteen implicit kicks of two evaluations each, since the relativistic layer forbids merging them: C26 Phase 3), so a year is 102,270 evaluations.

The vectorised routine computes every pair at once with numpy, the same formulas term for term, and masks the pairs the loop skips. Agreement with the
loops was 6e-16 relative on the first try, on all four served predictors, and stayed there through the rewrites that followed: the loop's Python
overhead was gone, but so much of what remained was numpy call overhead on eleven bodies that each saving had to be found by profiling. The
Moon's rotation model called np.cross twice per evaluation at 90 us each; the C22 figure looped over bodies in Python; the integrators rebuilt every
body as a frozen dataclass at every drift and kick, 35 us a body, a third of a force evaluation. Each of those was replaced by the same arithmetic in
cheaper form, and the evaluation came down to about 1 ms, the year to about 100 s under full load, the loops' 10 minutes. On an idle CPU it should be
near a minute; that was not measurable tonight and is recorded as not measured.

One real mistake on the way: the first live memo ran Forest-Ruth, not Yoshida-6, because the helper that picks the integrator falls back to the
predictor's own, and the served predictor's own is fr4. The receipt's evaluation count exposed it (six per step instead of twenty-eight).

## The Solution

body_position now reads the truth kernel (DE442) up to the edge of its fit data, taken as 2024-01-01, and for any later date integrates from the
kernel's state at that edge with the served predictor, Yoshida-6 at 0.1 d, under a time budget. If the budget runs out it says how far it got, what the
rest would cost, and that asking again continues; the one thing kept is a memo in the process, keyed by the physics fingerprint and the start, and
the answer says when it was used. Then the Captain added the part that turned a speed job into an exhibit: the time the calculation takes is to be
shown, not hidden. "You get to see it took ten minutes, and not a supercomputer's timeshare." So every live answer carries a receipt: the machine,
the wall time, the integrator and step, the force evaluations, the bodies and sources, the start and the lead, the memo, and the list of physics
layers with their sources and, where the C23b ablations measured it, the size of each layer for that body. Progress is reported to the client while
it runs. The sealed table was compared with the live path from the table's own start: Mars agrees to 16 cm at +5 yr, the Moon differs by 0.35 km in
the direction C19 predicted (the table's 0.025 d step carried a quarter of its Moon error as integration error; the live answer is nearer both DE440
and DE442).

## Key Learning

A served table is a cache of an old calculation; the live code is the thing to serve, and when it is slow the slowness is part of the answer, shown
with the work. And: a count of force evaluations in the receipt is a better test of which integrator ran than any label.
