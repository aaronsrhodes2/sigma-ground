## 1. Direct detection (LZ, XENONnT, PandaX-4T, DarkSide, SuperCDMS, CRESST, TESSERACT): limits, signals, neutrino fog

### Takeaway
The major direct-detection news of the window is the **LZ "event of interest"**, released 1–2 Sep 2026. LZ saw one nuclear-recoil-like event at **248 ± 23 (stat) ± 23 (sys) keV** in a 2.84 t·yr exposure. Its significance is **3.4σ local / 2.6σ global**, and it would imply WIMP masses **≳200 GeV** with a non-standard (momentum-dependent or inelastic) coupling. More than 25 theory papers followed within weeks, many pointing to ~1.1 TeV higgsinos with a mass splitting δ ≈ 300–500 keV, or to exothermic DM with δ ≈ 0.5–1 MeV. Elsewhere the window brought only incremental limits: XENONnT published its S2-only light-DM PRL and presented a 6.5 t·yr combined WIMP search, and DarkSide-50 published the first ultraheavy composite-DM search. No new full-exposure LZ spin-independent limit appeared in the window. The latest one is from Dec 2025.

### Cited Findings

**LZ extended-energy-window search: the 248 keV event (the headline result)**
- Title: "Search for dark matter particle interactions in an extended nuclear recoil energy window with the LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment". LZ Collaboration (D.S. Akerib + 216 authors; lead author S.R. Eriksen, Bristol). **arXiv:2609.02823** (submitted 2 Sep 2026). Presented at TeVPA 2026 (Japan) on 1 Sep 2026 and submitted to PRL — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02823); [Berkeley Lab press release](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2026/09/01/lz-sees-surprising-result-in-search-for-dark-matter/)
- Exposure **2.84 t·yr**: 220 live days, 27 Mar 2023 – 1 Apr 2024, in a 4.7 t fiducial volume. NR energy window **5.4–270 keV** — [alphaXiv summary of paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.02823); [LZ preprint PDF](https://lz.lbl.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/LZ_Preprint_260901_Dark_Matter_EFT_Nuclear_Recoil_Search_at_Higher_Energies.pdf)
- Event properties: E_R = **248 ± 23 (stat) ± 23 (sys) keV**, S1c = **540.1 phd**, S2c = **9268 phd**, recorded 21:22:39 UTC on 16 June 2023. It sits **1.5σ below the NR-band median** and **6.7σ below the ER-band median**, 26.4 cm above the cathode and 26.9 cm inside the TPC wall — [LZ preprint PDF](https://lz.lbl.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/LZ_Preprint_260901_Dark_Matter_EFT_Nuclear_Recoil_Search_at_Higher_Energies.pdf)
- Statistics: background-only p < 1.3×10⁻³ for a substantial subset of models (local >3σ). Maximum local significance is **3.4σ** (isoscalar NREFT L10 at m = 1000 GeV/c²). After the look-elsewhere correction over 616 model configurations (reduced to 293 distinguishable spectra) the global significance is **2.6σ**, about a 0.5% chance of a background fluctuation. The preferred masses are typically **>200 GeV/c²** — [LZ preprint PDF](https://lz.lbl.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/LZ_Preprint_260901_Dark_Matter_EFT_Nuclear_Recoil_Search_at_Higher_Energies.pdf); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.02823); [Berkeley Lab](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2026/09/01/lz-sees-surprising-result-in-search-for-dark-matter/)
- Caveats stated by the paper: the analysis was **non-blind** (salting did not cover the high-energy region), S1 pulse shape cannot conclusively separate ER from NR, and high-energy NR calibration near the event position is limited. The best background alternative is MSSI (multiple-scintillation single-ionization), whose model agrees with sideband data at p = 0.7 — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.02823)
- Background argument: a neutron needs **≥8 MeV** to give a xenon nucleus 250 keV in elastic scattering, and would almost always also produce lower-energy recoils — [LZ preprint PDF](https://lz.lbl.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/LZ_Preprint_260901_Dark_Matter_EFT_Nuclear_Recoil_Search_at_Higher_Energies.pdf)
- Status: a hint, not a discovery. LZ has kept taking data under the same field conditions since 1 Apr 2024, and the dataset is about one quarter of LZ's planned lifetime exposure. XENONnT spokesperson Aprile said the result "clearly requires a cross-check", and XENONnT is re-examining its own high-energy data — [Science News, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/dark-matter-supersymmetry-higgsino); [LZ preprint PDF](https://lz.lbl.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/LZ_Preprint_260901_Dark_Matter_EFT_Nuclear_Recoil_Search_at_Higher_Energies.pdf)
- **Hook:** the momentum transfer for a 248 keV Xe recoil is **q ≃ 246 MeV**. Elastic halo scattering therefore needs **m_χ ≳ 79 GeV**, and elastic scattering cannot explain why no lower-energy events accompany it — [Mahapatra & Paul, arXiv:2609.14799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14799)

**Theory responses to the LZ event (Sep–Oct 2026; all preprints, not peer reviewed)**
- Di Mauro, **arXiv:2609.02608** (2 Sep). A thermal pseudo-Dirac fermion at m_χ ≃ 1 TeV with relic-fixed **σ_N ≃ 6.5×10⁻⁴³ cm²** and **δ ≃ 297 keV** reproduces the event. A thermal higgsino has **m ≈ 1.1 TeV, δ ≃ 377 keV**. Elastic SI scattering fits poorly, while elastic SD O₄ remains viable — [arXiv:2609.02608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02608)
- Freese & Theodosopoulos, **arXiv:2609.01583** (1 Sep). A nearly pure higgsino with m ~ 1 TeV, inelastic through an off-diagonal Z coupling, approaches the LZ two-sided 90% interval for **δ ~ 350 keV** — [arXiv:2609.01583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01583). A follow-up dated 2 Oct by Freese and a co-author reportedly favours higgsinos of **~2,000 proton masses (~1.9 TeV)** with faster halo velocities and larger δ, which also suppresses solar neutrinos. I did not find its arXiv ID — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/dark-matter-supersymmetry-higgsino)
- Rodd, Safdi, Slatyer & Xu, **arXiv:2609.04175** (3 Sep). The rate and spectrum fit a thermal **1.1 TeV higgsino with δ ~ 370–490 keV**, depending on the velocity distribution. Such a higgsino would, however, generically produce more events in LZ's high-energy S1c sideband, where LZ reports none, so there is possible tension. A non-thermal **~500 GeV** higgsino may evade this — [arXiv:2609.04175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04175)
- Wu, Zhang & Zhu, **arXiv:2609.01590** (1 Sep). A 1.1 TeV higgsino with a few-hundred-keV splitting fits both the LZ event and a mild preference in 14 yr of Fermi-LAT Galactic Centre data. Together with the 10-yr IceCube solar-neutrino bound it predicts a GC gamma-ray line near H.E.S.S./CTAO sensitivity — [arXiv:2609.01590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01590)
- IceCube solar-neutrino constraint: Ramani and a colleague (2 Sep) argue that higgsinos captured in the Sun would have produced neutrinos IceCube has not seen. Nguyen, Linden & Hooper wrote a solar-neutrino paper on inelastic DM. I did not obtain arXiv IDs for either — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/dark-matter-supersymmetry-higgsino)
- de Lima, **arXiv:2609.05204** (4 Sep). Exothermic DM with **m_χ ~ 30–200 GeV and δ ~ 0.5–1 MeV** gives a single high-energy event without low-energy activity. Argon and germanium detectors should then see events above the current xenon window — [arXiv:2609.05204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05204)
- Gemmell, Hooper & Krnjaic, **arXiv:2609.26570** (22 Sep; FERMILAB-PUB-26-0702-T). A pseudo-Dirac fermion of **20–100 GeV** with a U(1)_B (baryon-number) vector mediator fits the event through exothermic χ₂→χ₁ down-scattering with **δ ~ 1000 keV** (global fit m_χ ~ 50 GeV). The same parameters fit the Galactic Center Excess. Their benchmark is m_χ = 35 GeV, δ = 1000 keV, g_B ~ 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵, giving N_ROI = 1 and N_sideband = 8×10⁻⁴. The rate scales as **A²** rather than Z² — [arXiv:2609.26570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26570)
- Mahapatra & Paul, **arXiv:2609.14799** (13 Sep). Endothermic inelastic spectra put only **6–17% of events below 150 keV**. Boosted-DM spectra with scalar or pseudoscalar–scalar operators put **99% / 92%** of events below 150 keV, and the pseudoscalar–pseudoscalar operator puts **75%** above. A handful more events would separate the scenarios — [arXiv:2609.14799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14799)
- Khan, Capozziello et al., **arXiv:2609.09230** (7 Sep). Elastic isovector L₆ᵛ is locally favoured at ~3.3σ. Xenon shell-model nuclear-response cancellations sit at **211 and 214 keV**, and in ⁴⁰Ar move to ~350 and 425 keV. At m_χ = 1 TeV the endothermic Ar/Xe recoil-scale ratio is **1.082**, versus **1.63–2.01** for nuclear-structure explanations, which gives a target-comparison test — [arXiv:2609.09230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09230)
- **[BACKGROUND]** DIAMX combined reanalysis, **arXiv:2512.05850** (Dec 2025). LZ and XENONnT both show NR-like excesses above 20 keV across 7.3 t·yr combined. Velocity-dependent or inelastic DM reaches local significance up to **4σ**, but the poorly known ¹²⁴Xe double-electron-capture charge yield can move this to below 1σ. Characteristic exothermic recoil energy: **E_R* = |δ|·m_DM/(m_DM + m_N)** — [arXiv:2512.05850](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05850v1)

### Inferences
- Hook check (my calculation): for a Xe-131 target (m_N ≈ 122 GeV) and v_max ≈ 800 km/s in the lab, the elastic kinematic limit E_R,max = 2μ²v²/m_N ≥ 248 keV needs μ ≳ 46 GeV, so m_χ ≳ 74 GeV. This is consistent with the ≳79 GeV in arXiv:2609.14799; the difference comes from the assumed v_max. A sigma-ground recoil-kinematics module can test against **q = √(2 m_N E_R) ≈ 246 MeV**.
- Hook check (my calculation): with the exothermic peak formula E_R* = |δ|·m_DM/(m_DM + m_N), the Gemmell–Hooper–Krnjaic benchmark (35 GeV, δ = 1 MeV) gives E_R* ≈ 222 keV, and m = 50 GeV gives ≈ 290 keV. Both are within the event's ±33 keV combined uncertainty.
- The theory papers disagree on the higgsino interpretation. Rodd et al. see sideband tension, Ramani and colleagues see IceCube tension, and Freese et al. reconcile both with ~1.9 TeV and faster halo speeds. Treat the "1.1 TeV higgsino, δ ≈ 350–490 keV" region as a contested benchmark, not an established fit.
- Several papers predict concrete target-dependence tests: the A² scaling of the exothermic U(1)_B model, events above xenon's window in argon or germanium, and the Ar/Xe recoil-scale ratio of 1.08 for excitation versus 1.6–2.0 for nuclear-structure explanations. These are clean discriminators that can be simulated.

## 2. Axions and ALPs (haloscopes, helioscopes, astrophysical bounds), plus ultralight scalar DM

### Takeaway
No axion discovery. The main window result is a CAPP/IBS haloscope paper (PRL, 10 Jul 2026) that **excluded a candidate signal at 1.036 GHz (≈4.28 μeV)** and reached near-DFSZ sensitivity over 1.026–1.045 GHz. Several prototypes added narrow new exclusions at 28.5 μeV and 42.4–43.2 μeV. BabyIAXO axion runs remain years away (2030+). For sigma-ground's σ field, the most relevant work constrains **ultralight scalar DM coupled to Standard Model constants**. Optical clocks (PTB) and the ²²⁹Th nuclear clock (JILA, early 2026) now bound α drift to ~10⁻¹⁹/yr and probe nuclear-sector scalar couplings at scales more than 10⁶ M_Planck.

## 3. Indirect detection (Fermi GCE, Totani 20 GeV halo, dwarfs, AMS-02, 3.5 keV line, neutrinos)

### Takeaway
The Fermi "20 GeV halo" claim (Totani, 2025) was **independently reproduced at pixel level** (arXiv:2607.08552, Jul 2026; TeVPA 2026). Best-fit values are m_χ ≈ 0.55–0.72 TeV and ⟨σv⟩ ≈ 1×10⁻²⁴ cm³ s⁻¹, about 4–5× above the dwarf limits (1.6–9.3× with systematics). A May–June 2026 dwarf reanalysis raised the stacked dwarf excess to **3.7σ local / 3.0σ global** at 100–200 GeV (bb̄), consistent with the GCE and the AMS-02 antiproton excess. A new XRISM Milky-Way-halo analysis (1 Oct 2026) sets the best keV-decay bounds near 3.5 keV, yet also finds a **~3σ local (2–2.5σ global) residual at 3.5311 keV**. Hooper and co-authors tied the GCE to the LZ event with a single inelastic model.

## 4. Structure and small-scale evidence (JWST/Euclid lensing, dwarfs, cusp-core, fuzzy DM, SIDM, streams, Bullet Cluster)

### Takeaway
The picture is mixed. JWST quad-lens analyses in June 2026 confirm CDM down to ~10⁷·² M☉ (thermal-relic WDM **m > 6.5–7.4 keV**). A September 2026 JWST analysis of 29 quads instead finds **Bayes factors of 6:1 to 24:1 favouring core-collapsed SIDM halos over CDM**. GD-1 stream kinematics show a **4σ** excess dispersion that prefers subhalos more compact than CDM predicts. The 10⁶ M☉ JVAS B1938+666 perturber (a 9σ concentration outlier for CDM) is now modelled as a core-collapsed SIDM halo or an FDM soliton with an SMBH. Euclid globular-cluster dynamics favour fuzzy DM with **m₂₂ ≈ 1–1.5**. New JWST work on the Bullet Cluster classifies it as a minor merger.

## 5. Primordial black holes as dark matter [PBH]

### Takeaway
No confirmed PBH detection. In July 2026 a hierarchical Bayesian OGLE-IV bulge analysis found that adding a PBH population is **favoured by the Bayes factor**, driven by the PBH velocity distribution of short-timescale, low-parallax events. The authors caution that Galactic-model deficiencies prevent ruling out non-PBH explanations. New gravitational-wave work using the scalar-induced GW background (LVK O1–O4a, 11 Sep 2026) constrains PBH formation "far below unity" without seeing a signal. Projected VLBI milli-lensing and Taiji constraints target the 10⁶–10⁹ M☉ and asteroid-mass windows. Pre-window hints remain unconfirmed: a sub-solar LVK candidate, HSC planet-mass events, and a lunar-mass LMC event.

### Cited Findings
- Perkins et al., "Microlensing Constraints on Primordial Black Holes toward the Bulge Are Galactic Model Limited", **ApJ 1006, 110 (published 17 Jul 2026)**, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ae66f6. ## 6. MOND and modified gravity vs. DM (wide binaries, RAR, a0, large-radius rotation curves) [MOD-GRAV]

### Takeaway
The wide-binary test is still split. Chae & Yoon (Jul 2026) claim a **>5σ MOND-type anomaly** (boost ~1.15–1.20 in median velocity; γ = G_eff/G_N ≈ 1.3–1.6 from earlier analyses). Cookson, Banik, El-Badry et al. (Feb 2026, background) find Newton **up to 1500× more likely**. The most notable new a0 result is MUSE-DARK III (A&A, May 2026). It finds the RAR acceleration scale **rises with redshift**: a0(z~1) = 2.38×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², a0(z) = 1.0 + 1.59z (×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²), ~30σ. A follow-up fit gives a0 ∝ (1+z)^0.78±0.15, consistent with tracking H(z). Several fringe or unreviewed claims (scalar "screening" in wide binaries, a "parameter-free" a0 = cH-based prediction) also appeared.

### Cited Findings

**Wide binaries (Gaia DR3)**
- Chae & Yoon, "Revisiting Data Quality Control and Multiple-star Modeling in Wide Binary Gravity Tests: Confirmation of MOND-type Gravitational Anomaly at Low Acceleration", **arXiv:2607.14450** (16 Jul 2026). They argue that proper quality control and triple-star modelling cannot remove the anomaly, which they confirm at **>5σ** (and **>3σ** in a sample 8× larger than Cookson et al.'s MOND-regime subsample, which had only **61** wide binaries). The anomaly matches realistic numerical QUMOND orbits. In the low-acceleration regime (g_N < 10⁻⁹ m s⁻²) the median ṽ at r_p/r_M > 1 is about **1.15–1.20×** Newtonian. Prior work found boost **γ ≡ G/G_N ≈ 1.3–1.6** (Chae 2024: ~1.4–1.5 at >5σ). They also claim Banik et al.'s Newtonian ⟨ṽ⟩ for α = 1 should be **0.55, not 0.64** — [arXiv:2607.14450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14450); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14450v1)
- **[BACKGROUND]** Cookson, Banik, El-Badry, Sutherland & Penoyre, "A Quality Framework for Testing Gravity with Wide Binaries: No Evidence for MOND", **arXiv:2602.24035** (Feb 2026). - **[BACKGROUND]** Pittordis, Sutherland & Shepherd (arXiv:2504.07569). In their MOND model, orbits at ≳10 kAU behave as Keplerian with **G boosted 1.36×** and **v boosted 1.17×**. **Radial acceleration relation and a0**

### Inferences
- Hook (my calculation): with H0 = 67.7 km/s/Mpc, **cH0/2π ≈ 1.05×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²**, close to the SPARC 1.20 ± 0.24 (sys). If a0 ∝ H(z) with Ωm = 0.307, a0(z=1)/a0(0) = H(1)/H0 ≈ 1.77. MUSE-DARK's linear fit gives ≈ 2.6 at z = 1, and the (1+z)^0.78 fit gives ≈ 1.72. A sigma-ground modified-gravity module can test whether its effective acceleration scale tracks H(z).

## 7. Scalar-field coupling, time- or environment-varying DM, and DM–DE unification [σ-FIELD]

### Takeaway
DESI DR2-era fits continue to show a mild preference for a **DM–DE interaction**: coupled quintessence with a field-dependent DM mass peaks at **|β| ~ 0.03** (no-coupling excluded at ~95% CL), and some fluid models reach 3–5σ. Two September 2026 analyses show this preference is **largely degenerate with dynamical dark energy (w0wa)** and that Bayesian evidence still favours ΛCDM. Separately, ultralight-scalar searches with clocks set strict limits on any oscillating or drifting constant (section 2). MUSE-DARK's rising a0(z) is the main "time-varying" galactic-dynamics claim (section 6). No experimental claim of DM varying with environment was found in the window, apart from unreviewed wide-binary "screening" claims.

### Cited Findings
- Yang, Zhang, Barbosa, Alvarez, Pan & Graef, "Do DESI-DR2 BAO data imply a coupling of dark matter and dark energy?", **arXiv:2609.05660** (4 Sep 2026). The interaction is **Q = Γ ρ_x** (constant Γ, independent of H). Combining Planck 2018, DESI DR2, and PantheonPlus/Union3/DESY5 supports evidence of interaction for **w_x = −1 and w_x < −1**, but **Bayesian evidence still favours ΛCDM**. For w_x > −1, S8 comes out mildly lower — [arXiv:2609.05660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05660)
- Wang, Yu & Wu, "Reassessing Evidence for Dark-Sector Interactions with Dynamical Dark Energy and DESI DR2", **arXiv:2609.03062** (2 Sep 2026). With **Q = βHρ_de** in w0waCDM (Planck+ACT CMB, DESI DR2, DES-Dovekie SNe), letting w evolve **substantially weakens the coupling preference**, and the interaction adds essentially no improvement in fit. Thawing and GEDE trajectories favour sizable couplings of opposite sign, while the mirage trajectory needs none. Growth signatures differ, which offers a discriminant — [arXiv:2609.03062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03062)

### Inferences
- [σ-FIELD] The closest observational analogue to a σ field that mediates a DM–DE coupling is the coupled-quintessence model with a field-dependent DM mass. Here the testable number is **|β| ≈ 0.03** (dimensionless fifth-force strength relative to gravity; fifth force ∝ 2β² of gravity in the standard convention, which is my addition, not from these sources). For energy-transfer forms, compare against **Q = Γρ_x** and **Q = βHρ_de**. Because the September 2026 papers show strong degeneracy with w0wa, sigma-ground should not treat the DESI interaction "hint" as established.
- [σ-FIELD] Any σ-field coupling to visible-sector constants must respect |α̇/α| ≲ 10⁻¹⁸ yr⁻¹ and the clock bounds on oscillating couplings across 10⁻²⁴–10⁻¹⁷ eV.
- Together, the LZ inelastic-DM interpretations (δ ~ 0.3–1 MeV splittings) and the 20 GeV halo's need for a sub-GeV mediator both point toward a **structured dark sector with light mediators**. Such a sector is often a scalar or vector field, a theme compatible with, but not evidence for, a σ-type scalar.
